Why this exists

Why does every new medium start by imitating the one it was supposed to replace? AI writes web pages, and we read them like paper.

Markdown has Obsidian: you read it and you edit it in the same place. HTML, the format every AI reaches for when it wants to show you something, has a browser tab. You can look. You cannot touch. A wrong number in a report means another prompt, another wait, and a new file that may have changed three other things while it was at it.

That is not an editing problem. It is a distribution problem, the same one Roy works on at Wozku: the moment someone wants to change or share what is in front of them, the tool should get out of the way. Retouch is that idea applied to a single file on your own disk.

It was built quickly, with an AI in the loop, on the pages it was meant to fix. That seemed like the only honest way to make it.

What it refuses to do

A tool that writes to your files earns trust by what it won't touch.

  • 1
    Nothing leaves your machine. No server, no account, no upload, no analytics. The extension asks for the minimum Chrome permits and nothing else.
  • 2
    Byte for byte. Only the text you changed is rewritten. Spacing, comments and scripts stay exactly as your AI left them.
  • 3
    Only what the page already knows. Colors, fonts and styles come from the page's own stylesheet, described in plain words. Nothing is invented, so nothing looks pasted in.
  • 4
    Honest about scripts. Text a script generates at load is refused rather than quietly corrupted.
  • 5
    Open. MIT licensed. Read it, fork it, keep it.
